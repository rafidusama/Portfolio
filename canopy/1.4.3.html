Foundations

An explainer has one job: leave the reader able to understand something they couldn't before, without needing to read anything else first. It doesn't need a scoop or a character. It needs structure, restraint with jargon, and — on this beat especially — a local angle that makes a global concept feel like it actually applies here.

Explainer Structure

What

State plainly what the thing is, in one or two sentences, before anything else.

Why

Explain why it matters right now — the hook that justifies the reader's time.

How

Walk through the mechanism or process, using the plainest language that's still accurate.

What's Next

Tell the reader what to watch for — a decision, a deadline, a follow-up event.

Avoiding Jargon Pile-Up

One technical term, defined once in plain language, is manageable. Three or four in the same paragraph, each requiring its own mental translation, is where readers give up. If a paragraph needs more than one term explained, consider whether the explainer needs a second paragraph instead.

Using Analogy Responsibly

Analogy That Misleads

"Carbon credits are like a gym membership you can trade" — captures the idea of a tradeable unit but implies effort-free exchange, which glosses over exactly the verification problems that make carbon markets contentious.

Analogy That Clarifies

"A carbon credit is like a receipt for pollution someone else agreed not to cause" — keeps the core mechanism (a claim standing in for an action) intact and doesn't hide the trust problem the story may go on to examine.

A good analogy preserves the specific tension the story needs the reader to understand. A bad one resolves that tension falsely, just to make the sentence easier to read.

Localizing a Global Explainer

A global concept like "loss and damage" or "carbon markets" means little to a reader until it's tied to a specific place, agency, or person they recognize. The localizing step isn't optional polish — it's usually the difference between an explainer that gets read and one that doesn't.
