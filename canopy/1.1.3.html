Foundations

In most Bangladeshi newsrooms, there is no dedicated environment desk. There's a reporter who was free that morning, or the one who covered a flood once and got tagged as "good with that stuff." Environmental journalism here is mostly done by generalists on rotation, not specialists — and the skills that follow have to work for that reality, not around it.

This isn't a workaround for a broken system. It's the actual shape of the job for most reporters who will ever touch this beat, and building habits for it is as core a skill as anything else in this toolkit.

The Constraint, Named Honestly

Multi-Beat Assignment

The same reporter covering a factory fire today may be assigned a climate policy story tomorrow, with no specialist handoff in between.

No Runway

Assignments often land same-day. There's rarely a week to read up before filing — the reporting and the learning happen at once.

The goal isn't to become an expert before you file. It's to know exactly what you still don't know, and to close the most important gaps fast.

Just Enough Context: A Two-Hour Checklist

  • Identify the one government body with jurisdiction over this issue, and whether they've said anything on record before.
  • Find one number that shows scale — how big, how many, how long — even a rough one, rather than filing with no quantification at all.
  • Ask whether this has happened before nearby, so you don't accidentally write it as a first-time event.
  • Identify one source who can speak for the people directly affected, not just officials or your own observation.
  • Flag, even to yourself, what you're still unsure about — so it doesn't quietly become an assumption in the copy.
