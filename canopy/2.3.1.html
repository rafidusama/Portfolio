Foundations

Every environmental reporter ends up explaining the same handful of concepts over and over — what a carbon credit is, how salinity intrusion works, what loss and damage means. Writing that explanation fresh every time wastes effort a reusable, well-maintained explainer bank avoids entirely.

Modular Structure for Reuse

An explainer built for reuse should be written in swappable sections — a paragraph on the mechanism, a paragraph on the current numbers, a paragraph on Bangladesh's specific stake — so a section can be lifted directly into breaking coverage without rewriting from scratch under deadline pressure. Writing it this way from the start costs a little more time up front and saves far more later.

Maintaining the Library

Version and Date Everything

Every saved explainer needs a "last verified" date. A number or policy detail from eighteen months ago may already be wrong.

One File, Findable Fast

A shared, searchable location beats scattered documents — the value of the bank depends entirely on being able to find the right piece under deadline pressure.

Keeping Explainers Current

Science and Policy Don't Stand Still

A perfectly accurate explainer written two years ago can become quietly wrong as policy changes, funding mechanisms evolve, or new research shifts a scientific consensus. Build a habit of reviewing high-use explainers on a schedule, not just when someone happens to notice an error.

"An explainer bank is only an asset if someone is responsible for keeping it true. Otherwise it's just old copy waiting to mislead someone."
