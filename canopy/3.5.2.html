Field Craft

Greenwashing rarely lies outright — it's usually built from technically true statements arranged to leave a stronger impression than the facts support. Spotting it is less about catching a lie and more about noticing what a claim carefully avoids saying.

Common Greenwashing Tactics

Vague Language

"Eco-friendly," "green," "sustainable" — words that sound substantive but commit to nothing measurable.

Selective Disclosure

Highlighting one genuinely positive initiative while staying silent on a much larger negative impact elsewhere in the same operation.

Irrelevant Claims

Touting compliance with a legal minimum as if it were a voluntary achievement — following the law isn't the same as going beyond it.

Imagery Over Substance

Green colors, leaves, and nature photography doing the persuasive work that specific data should be doing instead.

Evaluating a Sustainability Report

Look specifically for: a named baseline year to measure progress against, independent third-party verification or audit, and year-over-year figures that are actually comparable rather than redefined each cycle. A report with none of these three is mostly a communications document, whatever it calls itself.

Questions to Ask a PR Contact

"What's the specific number behind that claim, and what's it measured against?" "Has this been independently verified, and by whom?" "Does this figure cover your full operations and supply chain, or just one facility?" A company confident in its claim will usually have ready answers — hesitation itself is information.

"'Committed to sustainability' is a mission statement. 'Reduced emissions 12% against a 2020 baseline, verified by [named auditor]' is a claim you can actually check."
