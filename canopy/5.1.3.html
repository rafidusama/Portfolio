Module 5 · Ch.1 · 5.1.3

Field Craft

Covering disaster, displacement, and environmental harm asks something of the person doing the reporting, not just the story. Taking that seriously isn't a sign of not being cut out for the beat — it's part of doing the beat sustainably.

Vicarious Trauma in This Beat

Repeated exposure to others' loss, displacement, and disaster — even secondhand, through interviews and site visits rather than direct experience — can accumulate over time. Environmental and disaster reporting carries this risk in a way that's easy to underestimate because no single assignment feels like "the bad one."

Trauma-Informed Interviewing, From Both Sides

For the Source

Module 5.3 in Pillar 1 covers letting the person you're interviewing set the pace and stop at any point — worth revisiting from that angle.

For You

The same difficult material that affects the source can affect you. Debriefing after a hard interview isn't indulgent — it's part of doing the next one well.

Self-Care and Newsroom Support

A newsroom that treats difficult assignments as routine, with no space to acknowledge their weight, tends to lose good reporters to burnout rather than to a lack of skill. Simple practices — a colleague check-in after a hard trip, an editor who asks how you're doing and means it, rotating who covers the heaviest stories — make the beat sustainable over years, not just for one story.

"Taking this seriously is what lets you keep doing this work for years, not just get through this one assignment."

Field Tool

After-Assignment Check-In

This is a private reflection space, not an assessment or a diagnostic tool — it doesn't score, evaluate, or store anything anywhere. What you write here stays on this page and disappears when you close it. It's not a substitute for talking to a colleague, a supervisor, or a mental health professional if something is sitting heavily with you.

What's staying with you from this assignment, if anything?

Is there a specific moment or image you keep returning to?

What's one thing that would help you feel more settled right now?

However this assignment sits with you, that's a reasonable response to difficult material — there's no "right" amount to be affected by it.

If something from this is staying with you in a way that's hard to shake, it's worth mentioning to a colleague or supervisor, or reaching out to a mental health professional. That's a normal, sensible next step, not an overreaction.

Nothing you wrote above was saved or sent anywhere.
