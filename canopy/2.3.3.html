Field Craft

An idea is not a pitch. A pitch tells an editor exactly what you have, what you still need, and why it's worth their limited space — the difference between an idea getting assigned and an idea getting a vague "maybe, if you have time."

Pitch Structure

Angle

The specific claim or question, not just the topic.

Evidence in Hand

What you already have, concretely, right now.

Access Secured

Who has already agreed to talk, or what you still need to arrange.

Timeline

How long this realistically takes to report and file.

Format

Explainer, feature, or investigation — and why that one.

Anticipating Editor Objections

Editors in resource-constrained newsrooms are often weighing your pitch against a wire story that costs nothing and a beat reporter with less bandwidth than you'd like. Naming the likely objection yourself — "this will take longer than a typical piece, but here's why the payoff justifies it" — is usually more persuasive than leaving it unaddressed and hoping it doesn't come up.

Pitching a Systemic Story Past Resistance

"Isn't This Just a Feature About Poverty?"

A systemic or thematic pitch can get mistaken for a generic human-interest piece if the structural angle isn't stated up front. Lead with the specific mechanism or pattern your reporting will expose — not just the human story that will illustrate it — so the editor understands what makes this different from a piece they've already run.
