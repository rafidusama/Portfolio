Field Craft

Localizing an international story isn't a stylistic flourish you add at the end. It's a repeatable method — the same four steps every time — that turns a wire story nobody in Dhaka will click into one that tells a reader something about their own life.

The Localization Framework

1

Find the Mechanism

What is actually causing the global phenomenon, in concrete terms?

2

Find the Local Equivalent

Does that same mechanism operate here, even under a different name?

3

Find the Local Data

What specific number or record shows this mechanism is active locally?

4

Find the Local Source

Who can speak to the local version, on record, with authority?

Skipping step three is the most common shortcut, and it's the one that turns a localized story into a forced one — a local quote bolted onto an unchanged global narrative, rather than a story that's actually about here.

Common Localization Traps

Forced Localization

A global heatwave story gets a single quote from a Dhaka resident saying "yes, it's hot here too," with no local data, no local mechanism, no real connection beyond the shared weather.

Genuine Localization

The same heatwave story identifies the specific local mechanism — urban heat island effect in a named low-canopy neighborhood — with local temperature data and a resident whose daily routine has measurably changed because of it.
