Field Craft

Digital security isn't just for investigative reporters chasing organized crime. Environmental reporting regularly challenges powerful commercial and political interests, and a source's safety can depend on habits that take minutes to build but are easy to skip under deadline pressure.

Secure Communication Practices

Encrypted Messaging

Signal or similarly encrypted apps protect message content in transit — a meaningful upgrade over standard SMS or unencrypted email for sensitive exchanges.

Separate Sensitive Threads

Keep highly sensitive source conversations in a distinct, minimal-history thread rather than mixed into routine chat — it limits exposure if a device is ever compromised.

Protecting Source Identity

Notes and files are often the weakest link, not the messaging app. A source's name written in a notebook that gets left in a taxi is as much a security failure as a hacked phone — think about where identifying information physically and digitally lives, not just how it's transmitted.

When the Story Challenges Power

Reporting that threatens a powerful commercial or political interest raises the stakes on every one of these habits — the same carelessness that's low-risk on a routine story can expose a source to real retaliation on a story with real financial or political consequences for someone powerful.
