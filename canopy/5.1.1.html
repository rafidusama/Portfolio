Module 5 · Ch.1 · 5.1.1

Foundations

Physical safety planning isn't a bureaucratic add-on before a trip — it's part of the reporting itself. A story you can't safely gather is a story you can't tell, and the planning that keeps you safe is usually quick if it's built in from the start rather than improvised at the last minute.

The 3 P's Framework

Prepare

Research the site, the people, and the specific risks before you leave — know what you're walking into.

Protect

Have the right equipment, contacts, and communication plan in place for the specific risk profile.

Prevent

Build in the habits — check-ins, exit plans, shared itineraries — that stop a manageable problem from becoming a crisis.

Site-Specific Risk Assessment

An industrial site carries different risks than a disaster zone, which carries different risks than a politically sensitive location — physical hazard, social tension, and legal exposure each vary by site type, and a generic safety checklist misses what actually matters for the specific place you're going.

Emergency Contact Protocols

Before any assignment with elevated risk, share your itinerary and expected check-in times with someone specific who knows what to do if you miss a check-in — not just "someone at the office," but a named person with a clear next step.

Field Tool

Field Safety Self-Assessment

Answer honestly about the assignment ahead of you. Get a tailored pre-trip checklist and an overall risk flag.
