Field Craft

You don't need advanced statistics to find a lead in a dataset. A trend, an outlier, and a rough sense of whether two things move together will surface most of what's actually reportable — the skill is knowing which pattern is worth chasing and which is just noise.

Trends, Outliers, and Anomalies

Trend

A sustained direction over time — this month's reading being higher than last month's isn't a trend on its own, but a consistent rise across many periods is.

Outlier

A single value far outside the normal range — worth investigating specifically, since it could be a real event, a data error, or a measurement anomaly.

Correlation Is Not Causation

Two Things Moving Together Isn't Proof One Caused the Other

If pollution readings and hospital admissions rise together, that's a real, reportable pattern worth investigating — but it's not by itself proof that one caused the other. Both could be driven by a third factor, like a weather pattern. Report the correlation honestly as a pattern to investigate, not as a proven causal claim, until you have more than the numbers alone.

Lead vs. Noise

A single unusual data point in a long, otherwise stable series might be a lead. The same unusual point in a dataset that jumps around constantly might just be normal noise for that particular measurement. Before chasing an outlier as a story, check how much the data typically varies — an outlier only means something relative to how stable the baseline usually is.

"The pattern is the tip. The reporting is still the part that tells you what it's the tip of."
