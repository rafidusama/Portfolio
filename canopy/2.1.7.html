Field Craft

These three beats are less established than water, air, or the Sundarbans, which means the sourcing habits and baseline framing are less standardized — exactly why getting the fundamentals right early makes for a stronger, more differentiated beat.

Waste Management and the Informal Economy

A large share of Bangladesh's actual recycling happens through an informal waste-picking economy that official statistics frequently miss entirely. Any story citing a "recycling rate" should specify whether that figure includes informal collection — it usually doesn't, which can make formal-sector numbers misleadingly low or high depending on what's being compared.

Energy Transition Pressures

Bangladesh's energy transition stories typically involve a genuine three-way trade-off between energy security, cost, and emissions commitments — a renewable project that looks straightforwardly positive in isolation may still compete with, rather than replace, planned gas or coal capacity. Understanding what a specific project is actually offsetting is more useful than treating any renewable announcement as unambiguous progress.

Climate Impact on Food Security

Salinity, heat stress, and shifting rainfall patterns connect directly to national food security policy — a crop yield story is rarely just an agriculture story, since it usually implicates the same climate and water pressures covered elsewhere in this toolkit.

Urban Heat as an Emerging Beat

Rising urban heat, concentrated in dense, low-green-cover neighborhoods, is an emerging public health beat distinct from the broader climate story — it has its own data (local temperature readings, heat-related illness records) and its own affected populations, often outdoor workers and low-income residents with limited cooling access.

"These are the beats your competitors haven't fully staked out yet. That's an opportunity, not a gap in importance."
