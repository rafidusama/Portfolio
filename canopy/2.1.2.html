Foundations

Dhaka's air quality is a national story that happens to be concentrated in one city, and it rarely has a single cause. Brick kilns, vehicle emissions, industrial sources, and construction dust tend to overlap in the same neighborhoods, which is exactly why a single-cause headline is usually an oversimplification.

Brick Kiln Seasonality and Enforcement Gaps

Kiln pollution is strongly seasonal, worsening in the dry season when kilns run at full capacity, and seasonal bans on unlicensed kiln operation are common but unevenly enforced. A story that checks whether a specific ban is actually being enforced this season, rather than assuming it is, is usually the more useful story.

Vehicle and Industrial Emissions

Vehicle emissions and industrial sources compound kiln smoke in dense urban corridors, which makes single-source blame — "it's the kilns" or "it's the traffic" — usually incomplete. Checking which sources dominate in a specific season and specific location produces a more defensible story than a general claim about Dhaka's air.

Narayanganj as an Industrial Hotspot

Narayanganj's concentration of dyeing, textile, and other industrial facilities makes it a recurring hotspot distinct from Dhaka's more traffic-and-kiln-driven pollution profile — a useful example of why "air quality in the capital region" often needs to be reported district by district rather than as one undifferentiated story.

Acute vs. Chronic Stories

Acute Story

A specific smog event, a sudden spike tied to a weather pattern or a specific incident — time-bound, news-driven, usually needs same-day framing.

Chronic Story

The annual AQI trend, cumulative health impact, and enforcement gaps over years — needs historical data and is less bound to a single news hook.

Don't Read a Chronic Problem Off One Bad Day

A single day's poor reading is a data point, not a trend — check whether it fits a known seasonal pattern before framing it as either a crisis or an anomaly.
