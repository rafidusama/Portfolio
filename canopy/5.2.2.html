Field Craft

Bangladesh's cybercrime legislation has changed names and provisions several times in less than a decade, and each version has faced documented concerns about vague language being used against journalists rather than genuine cybercriminals. Knowing the current law and its lineage matters, because the specific provisions in force shift.

The Legislative Timeline

2006
ICT Act
Original cybercrime law, later widely criticized
2018
Digital Security Act (DSA)
Replaced ICT Act; documented heavy use against journalists
2023
Cyber Security Act (CSA)
Replaced DSA; retained many of the same provisions
2025
Cyber Security Ordinance
Repealed CSA; removed some provisions, retained others
2026
Cyber Protection Act
Current law, passed by parliament 10 April 2026

Timeline based on reporting from the Clooney Foundation for Justice's TrialWatch initiative, the Committee to Protect Journalists, and Bangladeshi press coverage as of mid-2026. Legislation in this area has changed repeatedly and may change again — verify the current text before relying on any specific provision.

Documented Patterns of Use Against Journalists

Vague, Broad Provisions

A TrialWatch review of 222 cases under the Digital Security Act found it was frequently used against journalists and critics of powerful figures, with provisions broad enough to criminalize ordinary reporting and criticism.

Mass Filings and Case-Stacking

CPJ has documented the use of First Information Reports naming large numbers of people, sometimes unidentified, later used to implicate individual journalists — along with concerns about "case-stacking," where multiple overlapping cases burden a single reporter.

What Carried Over

Successive laws have repealed their predecessors while retaining some of the same vague language. Provisions criminalizing content that "creates anxiety" or relates broadly to "communal hate speech," for example, have been flagged by legal researchers as retaining the same interpretive risk that made earlier versions controversial — worth checking specifically in whatever version of the law is current when you're reporting.

Practical Risk-Reduction Steps

Keep a factual, well-documented record of your reporting process specifically because vague provisions can be interpreted broadly — being able to show your work quickly is protective regardless of which specific law is in force. If a story touches a subject where these laws have previously been invoked, loop in an editor before publication, not after a complaint arrives.
