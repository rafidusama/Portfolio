A permit tells you what was allowed. A financial trail tells you who actually benefits from what happened next — and it's often the harder, more durable story, because permits get revoked while money keeps moving.

Company Registry Basics

RJSC

The Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms is Bangladesh's central company registry, under the Ministry of Commerce. Free basic search returns company name, registration number, address, incorporation date, and entity type.

Two Separate Identifiers

A company's RJSC registration number confirms legal existence; its ETIN (issued separately by the National Board of Revenue) confirms tax registration. A complete profile checks both.

Certified copies and deeper filing history (directors, shareholders, annual returns) typically require a paid request through RJSC directly — budget both time and a modest fee for this.

Procurement and Tender Records

Bangladesh's central e-procurement system publishes government tender records, giving you a way to see which company won a specific contract, at what value, and under which agency — a natural companion to an environmental permit, since infrastructure and industrial projects often involve both a permit and a public contract.

Linking Beneficiaries to Environmental Harm

The chain usually runs: a company holds a permit or contract → the company has registered directors and shareholders → those individuals or parent entities are the actual financial beneficiaries. Cross-referencing a facility's permit holder against RJSC's director records is how you move from "this company operates the facility" to "these specific people benefit from it operating."

See Pillar 5, Module 1.2 (Digital Security & Source Protection) for safe handling of sensitive financial documents and sources.

"The permit expires. The ownership structure usually doesn't — that's often where the real accountability story lives."
