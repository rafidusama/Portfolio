Field Craft

International organizations and donors move a lot of the money behind Bangladesh's climate adaptation work, and they also move a lot of the framing — a press release from a development bank describes its own project in the best available light, which is a different thing from an independent assessment of whether it worked.

The Major Players

Multilateral Development Banks

The World Bank and the Asian Development Bank (ADB) are among the largest sources of climate-related lending to Bangladesh, financing everything from embankments to policy-based loans tied to climate reform commitments.

UN Agencies

UNDP provides technical assistance on climate finance planning and governance; other UN bodies including UNCDF and ILO run more specific programs, from local adaptation financing to labor-focused coordination forums.

Bilateral Donors

Countries including the UK (via FCDO), Japan (via JICA), France (via AFD), and Sweden (via Sida) fund adaptation and resilience programs directly, often alongside multilateral institutions on the same initiatives.

Dedicated Climate Funds

The Green Climate Fund (GCF) is a major multilateral fund specifically for climate projects in developing countries, channeling finance that's distinct from general development lending.

Being Alert to Donor Framing

Whose Success Story Is It?

A donor-funded program's own communications will describe it in terms of impact and success — that's the nature of institutional communications, not necessarily dishonesty. Treat a donor's project description the way you'd treat a company's sustainability claim: a starting point for independent verification, not a finding to report as-is.

"The size of the loan or grant is a fact. Whether the money achieved what it was supposed to is a separate question, and usually the more important one."
