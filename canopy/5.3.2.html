Advanced

Environmental harm falls disproportionately on people with the least power to shape how their own story gets told. This module extends the interview-specific guidance in Pillar 1 to the wider ethical questions around representing a vulnerable community across an entire story, not just a single conversation.

Avoiding Extractive Reporting Relationships

An extractive relationship takes a community's story, quotes, and images to build a reporter's or outlet's work, then leaves with nothing given back — no copy of the published piece, no follow-up, no acknowledgment beyond the transaction itself. Treating a community as a source relationship rather than a one-time extraction changes decisions throughout a story, not just at the interview stage.

Consent and Follow-Up Obligations

Consent Isn't Just at the Start

Circling back after a story runs — sharing the published piece, checking whether anyone experienced consequences from participating — is part of the ethical obligation, not an optional courtesy.

Follow-Up Has Real Cost

In a resource-constrained newsroom, follow-up is easy to skip. Building it into your process from the start, even briefly, is what separates ethical practice from good intentions.

Representation vs. Reduction to Victimhood

Reduction

A community appears only as passive victims of circumstance — described entirely through loss and hardship, with no trace of agency, opinion, or ordinary life beyond the crisis.

Representation

The same community appears as people with views, decisions, and complexity, who are also experiencing genuine hardship — the hardship is real and present, but it isn't the entirety of who they are on the page.
