Foundations

Government agencies aren't the only credible sources on the beat, and often aren't the fastest. A well-mapped set of research institutions and advocacy organizations fills the gaps official channels leave — as long as you're clear-eyed about which kind of source you're talking to.

Research vs. Advocacy

Research Institution

Aims to produce findings independent of a predetermined policy position — useful as relatively neutral data and analysis, though still worth checking funding sources.

Advocacy Organization

Works explicitly toward a specific policy or legal outcome — valuable for expertise and access, but their framing of an issue is not a neutral baseline and should be attributed as advocacy, not presented as settled fact.

Neither type is inherently more trustworthy — a research institution can have subtle funder influence, and an advocacy group can have rigorous, citable data. The point is knowing which kind of claim you're getting, and sourcing balance accordingly.
