Foundations

Water is the oldest beat in Bangladesh and the one least likely to run out of stories. Rivers here are contested space — encroached on, dammed, dredged, silted, sued over — and the pattern behind any single water story is almost always bigger than the story itself.

River Grabbing and Encroachment Patterns

Encroachment usually follows a recognizable sequence: informal settlement or structure appears on a riverbank or floodplain, official notice is issued or ignored, the structure becomes permanent through inaction, and eventually either a court order or a disaster forces the issue back onto the agenda. Recognizing which stage a specific encroachment is in tells you what kind of story it currently is — an early-warning tip, an accountability piece about inaction, or a court-ordered eviction story.

The Buriganga as a Recurring Case Study

The Buriganga has been a recurring subject of sustained Bangladeshi environmental coverage for years, with reporting on encroachment and industrial effluent contributing to periodic court interventions and demarcation drives. It's a useful case study precisely because it shows how sustained, repeated coverage — not a single story — is often what moves a river conflict from "known problem" to "acted upon."

Water Governance Actors

BWDB

The Bangladesh Water Development Board manages embankments, drainage, and much of the country's water infrastructure — often your first call for technical questions about a specific river or embankment.

River Tribunals

Dedicated river-related tribunals and court proceedings periodically rule on encroachment and pollution cases — checking whether a specific river has an active tribunal case is a fast way to find a legal angle.

Seasonal Water Stories

Dry-Season Stories

Water scarcity, shrinking rivers, salinity intrusion becoming more visible, and irrigation competition tend to dominate the dry season, roughly November through March.

Monsoon-Season Stories

Flooding, embankment failure, erosion, and displacement dominate the monsoon months, roughly June through September — the same river can be two completely different stories six months apart.
