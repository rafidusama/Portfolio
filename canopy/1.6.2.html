Module 1 · Ch.6 · 1.6.2

Advanced

Accountability reporting and solutions reporting are often treated as opposites — one names what's wrong, the other names what's working. The strongest long-form environmental features do both in the same piece, and the craft is in the sequencing: naming the systemic failure clearly enough that a solution doesn't read as an excuse for it.

Sequencing Problem and Solution

A common structure establishes the human cost first, then the systemic and structural causes, then the response — in that order. Reversing it, leading with the solution before the reader understands the scale of the problem, tends to undersell both halves: the solution looks more complete than it is, and the problem looks like it's already being handled.

Avoiding Solutions That Let Responsible Parties Off the Hook

Whose Story Is the Solution, Really?

If the entity responsible for a problem is also the one presenting the "solution," scrutinize whether the fix addresses the root cause or just manages its symptoms. A livelihoods program for people harmed by a systemic failure is valuable and worth reporting — but it isn't a substitute for accountability reporting on why the systemic failure exists in the first place. Good dual-framed stories keep both threads visible.

"A solution earns its place in the story by coexisting with the problem, not by replacing it."
