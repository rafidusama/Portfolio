Field Craft

Air quality data is some of the most accessible environmental data available — the skill isn't finding it, it's knowing how to query it precisely and read what you get back correctly.

Real-Time vs. Historical Data

Real-Time Queries

Platforms like OpenAQ return the most recent measurements from monitoring stations, useful for covering an acute event as it happens.

Historical Queries

The same platforms hold historical records, letting you compare a current reading against the same period last year — essential for distinguishing an acute spike from a chronic trend.

Official Stations vs. Citizen Sensors

Official government monitoring stations are fewer in number but more rigorously calibrated; citizen sensor networks are denser but vary more in accuracy. Combining both gives you broader spatial coverage while still anchoring your headline number to the more rigorously maintained official readings.

Reading Spatial and Temporal Variation

One Station Isn't the Whole City

Air quality varies significantly by neighborhood and even by street — a single monitoring station's reading may not represent conditions a few kilometers away. Similarly, time of day and season shift readings dramatically. Always specify which station, and when, a number came from.

"'Dhaka's AQI was 180 today' is a headline. 'The Darus Salam station recorded 180 at 9am, typical for a winter morning' is a story someone can actually verify."
