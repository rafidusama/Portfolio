Field Craft

A dramatic photo or video circulating online during an environmental incident is exactly the kind of material that gets shared before anyone checks it — which is exactly why it needs checking before it goes anywhere near your story.

Reverse Image Search

Google Images

Upload or paste an image URL to find where else it's appeared online, and when.

TinEye

Specializes in finding the earliest known instance of an image across the web.

Yandex Images

Often surfaces different results than Google, especially for images from the region.

Geolocation Verification Basics

Match visible landmarks, road signage, building architecture, and terrain features against known reference imagery for the claimed location. Shadow direction and length can help confirm or contradict a claimed time of day. None of these alone is conclusive — the strength comes from several details agreeing with each other.

Identifying Manipulated or Recycled Media

A reverse image search that surfaces the same image from years earlier, or from a different country, is the single most common way old or unrelated media gets exposed as being recirculated under a new, false claim. Inconsistent lighting, mismatched shadows, or a visible watermark from an unrelated outlet are also worth checking for.

"The most common fake isn't a sophisticated forgery — it's a real photo from somewhere else, relabeled."
