A Narrative Profile

Bridging the gap between Academic Rigor and Public Narrative.

Usama Rafid is a storyteller at heart, a journalist by trade, and an academic by vocation. Based in Dhaka, his work explores the intersection of socio-economic shifts and human narratives.

The Journey of the Word

Born and raised in the vibrant chaos of Dhaka, Usama Rafid’s fascination with storytelling began within the hallowed halls of the University of Dhaka. It was here, amidst the historic architecture and the intellectual fervor of the campus, that he honed his ability to deconstruct complex societal structures into relatable human experiences.

His professional odyssey took flight at Roar Bangladesh, where he spent over half a decade pioneering long-form explanatory journalism. As a lead contributor, he mastered the art of "narrative non-fiction," turning data-heavy research into compelling sagas that reached millions. This commitment to clarity and depth continued at The Business Standard (TBS), where he bridged the gap between dry economic analysis and the lived realities of the Bangladeshi people.

"Journalism is the first draft of history; academia is the second. I find myself most at home in the space between the two."

Today, Usama balances his editorial projects with his role as a Lecturer at ULAB. In the classroom, he challenges the next generation of communicators to look beyond the headline and seek the underlying pulse of the story. His teaching philosophy is rooted in the same principles that guide his writing: intellectual honesty, relentless curiosity, and a deep respect for the reader's intelligence.
