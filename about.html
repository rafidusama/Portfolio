Career Narrative

Usama Rafid grew up in Khulna and came to Dhaka to study, and stayed to work. He holds a BSS and MSS in Mass Communication and Journalism from the University of Dhaka, where he earned academic performance scholarships across multiple years, including the Tofazzol Hossain Scholarship for the highest CGPA in his freshman, sophomore, and junior years, and the Sitara Parvin Prize for outstanding results at the Bachelor's level.

He spent over five years at Roar Bangla writing and producing long-form explanatory content before moving into feature journalism at The Business Standard, where he reported on local communities, media economics, and the changing news landscape. He also co-founded Coastal Voices, a U.S. State Department-funded platform for climate and coastal community storytelling.

In parallel, he has worked as a researcher at the BRAC Institute of Governance and Development (BIGD), where he was selected for the Young Researchers' Fellowship in the Qualitative Research Stream, and at the Press Institute Bangladesh (PIB), where his work focused on information disorder and media analysis using OSINT techniques.

Today, Usama teaches at the University of Liberal Arts Bangladesh (ULAB), where he works with undergraduate and postgraduate students on journalism practice, media theory, environmental communication, and the history of Bangladeshi media. His research sits across journalism studies, media economics, and the sociology of news, with a particular interest in how journalism survives, adapts, and sometimes fails in Bangladesh and South Asia.
