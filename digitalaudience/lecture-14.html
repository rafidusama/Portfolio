A Bangladeshi news outlet gains 100,000 new Facebook followers in a month. Has its audience grown?

Only if those followers return, read, share, and trust. Reach is not relationship. Growth is not development.

The foundational distinction

Growth vs development

Audience growth

More people

Increasing the raw number of people who have ever encountered your content. Measured in reach, followers, page views, downloads, impressions.

  • Can be bought (paid promotion, viral campaigns)
  • Does not guarantee repeat visits or trust
  • Platform algorithms can deliver it and withdraw it
  • What most dashboards measure by default
Audience development

Deeper relationships

Converting casual readers into loyal returners, then into active community members. Measured by return visits, subscription conversion, referrals, engagement depth.

  • Cannot be bought, only earned over time
  • Survives platform algorithm changes
  • Produces higher LTV and lower churn
  • What most dashboards fail to measure adequately
Carlson (2018) · Nelson (2018)

The engagement editor and the elusive metric

  • What they do: mediate between analytics data and editorial decision-making. They hold the audience in view when journalists make story choices.
  • Hearken's approach: engagement as an ongoing process of actively listening to audiences to earn their trust and loyalty. "Journalists better serve audiences when they focus on how audiences actually respond."
  • The measurement problem: audience engagement still lacks an agreed definition, let alone a metric. Hearken could not quantify the benefit of its approach. Its sales pitch relied on appeals to intuition, not data.
  • The structural constraint: journalism's market information regime rewards size (which is measurable) over trust (which is not). Organisations face economic incentives to pursue audience growth rather than audience development because they can measure the former.

The lesson: what gets measured gets managed. If trust cannot be measured, it tends not to be managed. The engagement editor's job is to make trust legible to an organisation that runs on numbers.

Routledge Companion to Media Audiences

Parasocial relationships as loyalty infrastructure

  • The structure: the audience member feels intimacy, familiarity, and loyalty toward a creator or journalist. The connection seems reciprocal, though it is not. The audience can choose to participate in relationships on offer but cannot initiate them.
  • Why it produces loyalty: parasocial connection functions like friendship: it reduces churn, increases sharing, motivates subscription, and makes audiences resilient to competitive alternatives.
  • Digital amplification: YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram allow audiences to comment, get responses, and feel individually noticed. Perceived reciprocity increases parasocial bond strength even when the creator cannot actually respond to everyone.
  • Bangladesh example: Bangladeshi street food creators who reply to comments, remember regulars, and create "inside" references build audiences that follow them across platform changes. The relationship, not the content, is the retention mechanism.
Strategic imperative

Own the relationship, not just the reach

  • Email newsletters: the publisher owns the subscriber contact. Unlike Facebook followers, an email list is a direct relationship. The newsletter renaissance is partly a strategic hedge against platform dependency.
  • Subscription: a paying subscriber has made an active commitment. They have the lowest churn, highest LTV, and are least likely to leave because of a competitor's algorithm recommendation.
  • Communities (Discord, WhatsApp groups, forums): a community around content is harder to replicate than the content itself. A creator can migrate platforms; a community can migrate with them.
  • The Bangladeshi opportunity: most Bangladeshi publishers have built strategies around Facebook distribution without owning the audience relationship. Building owned channels that the algorithm cannot mediate is the strategic priority for the next decade.

Prothom Alo's email newsletter list and Chorki's direct subscription app are both more durable assets than their Facebook page follower counts, because they survive any algorithm change.

Carlson (2018) · Critical perspective

Reciprocal vs paternalistic audience relations

Not all audience engagement is equal. Carlson distinguishes two fundamentally different stances organisations can take toward their audiences.

Reciprocal engagement

The journalist listens

The organisation genuinely responds to what audiences say they need. Editorial decisions are influenced by audience input, not just audience data. The relationship goes two directions.

  • Hearken model: ask what audiences want to know before assigning stories
  • Community journalism: communities co-define coverage priorities
  • Result: higher trust, harder to replicate by competitors
Paternalistic engagement

The organisation knows best

The organisation gives audiences what the editors think is good for them. Engagement tools are used to increase stickiness, not to change what is made. The relationship is one-directional.

  • Push notifications designed to maximise click-through
  • Recommendation algorithms that surface more of what you already chose
  • Result: efficient at scale, but produces the engagement-content tension Carlson describes
Branching scenario · Growth strategy

Grow fast or grow deep?

You lead audience development at a Bangladeshi OTT platform with 200,000 subscribers. Your board wants to double subscribers in 12 months. You have a budget for one primary strategy.
Option A outcome. You hit 420,000 subscribers by month 10. Churn spikes to 18% monthly as viral arrivals find nothing to hold them. By month 18 you are back at 230,000. The board is unhappy. Growth without development is a leaking bucket.
Option B outcome. Subscriber growth slows to 8% monthly. But churn falls from 12% to 4%. LTV per subscriber rises 40%. By month 18 you have 310,000 subscribers who stay, talk about the platform, and convert friends. The board gets a stronger business even though the headline subscriber number disappoints.
Option C outcome. You build 90,000 email subscribers over 12 months. 22% convert to paid in month 13 after a targeted offer. You now have 240,000 paid subscribers plus a 70,000-strong direct channel you own regardless of what Facebook or YouTube do. Slower than A, but the audience is yours.

Knowledge check

Nelson's study of Hearken found that newsrooms struggled to adopt audience engagement because it lacked an agreed metric. What does this reveal about journalism's market information regime?

C. Nelson applies structuration theory: the regime privileges audience size (measurable) over trust (not yet measurable). What gets measured gets managed; what cannot be measured tends to be deprioritised regardless of its importance.
